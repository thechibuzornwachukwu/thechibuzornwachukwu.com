Biography

Short

Chibuzor Nwachukwu is an AI Engineer, AI Product Builder, and emerging AI Governance professional focused on building practical AI systems and exploring how artificial intelligence should be developed, deployed, and governed.

He works across AI engineering, product development, software systems, and technology strategy, with a particular interest in AI agents, trustworthy AI, and the application of emerging technologies to real-world problems in Africa.

Chibuzor is the builder of Quotla, a business management platform designed to help African small businesses manage their operations with greater confidence. He has also worked on AI and machine learning projects including Valor, an award-winning agricultural AI solution.

He holds a Bachelor's degree in Computer Science from Caleb University and has received multiple academic and leadership recognitions, including being selected as Vice-Chancellor for One Day.

Long

Chibuzor Joshua Nwachukwu is an AI Engineer, AI Product Builder, and emerging AI Governance professional focused on building practical artificial intelligence systems and examining the systems, institutions, and policies required to deploy them responsibly.

His work sits at the intersection of artificial intelligence, software engineering, product development, and technology strategy. He is particularly interested in AI agents, machine learning, trustworthy AI, AI governance, and the implications of increasingly accessible AI systems for businesses, governments, and individuals in emerging markets.

Professionally, Chibuzor works in AI and ML engineering, where he contributes to the design and development of AI-powered systems. His experience includes building backend systems, developing AI functionality, deploying services, and translating complex technical requirements into usable products.

He is also the builder of Quotla, a business management platform designed for African small businesses. Quotla focuses on helping business owners manage areas such as quotations, invoices, expenses, inventory, customers, and business records through a more accessible digital experience. His work on the product combines software engineering, AI integration, product strategy, and user research.

Earlier in his career, Chibuzor worked on Valor, an AI-powered agricultural solution developed with a team of Caleb University students. The system used machine learning and image processing to help identify the ripeness of mangoes, addressing challenges faced by farmers, suppliers, and consumers. The team won first place and the Most Innovative Solution award at the Bells Tech Hackathon in 2025.

His interest in AI extends beyond building applications. His undergraduate thesis, "Adversarial Attacks on Retrieval Augmented Generation-based Large Language Models," explored the security and reliability of modern AI systems. He has continued to explore questions around AI safety, model reliability, governance, and the consequences of increasingly capable AI.

Chibuzor earned a Bachelor's degree in Computer Science from Caleb University with a CGPA of 4.64/5.0. During his undergraduate years, he appeared on the Vice-Chancellor's Honour List three times and received three Vice-Chancellor's Commendations. In December 2024, he was honoured as Vice-Chancellor for One Day, giving him the opportunity to participate in the university's leadership activities at the highest institutional level.

He also served as President of the National Association of Computing Students at Caleb University, where he led initiatives within the computing student community and developed experience in organisational leadership, communication, and technology advocacy.

Chibuzor's public work increasingly focuses on the question of what happens when powerful AI becomes accessible to almost everyone. He is particularly interested in AI governance, responsible AI deployment, AI agents, and the gap between the speed at which technology develops and the ability of institutions to govern its use.

He brings a practical perspective to these conversations because his work spans both sides of the problem: building AI systems and thinking about the structures required to make those systems useful, safe, accountable, and appropriate for the people who use them.

His broader ambition is to contribute to the development of AI systems and policies that allow emerging economies, particularly African countries, to participate meaningfully in the next generation of technological development.
